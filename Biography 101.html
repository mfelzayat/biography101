Course No. 101 · The Craft of Biography

Every life is a draft waiting to be told.

A short, practical course on writing honest, vivid biographies — from the first interview to the final paragraph. No prior writing required, just curiosity and a notebook.

Format
Six self-paced modules
Time
~8 hours total
Tools
Notebook, recorder, one patient subject
Prerequisite
None
01 — Syllabus

Biography is listening, shaped into narrative.

This course treats biography as a form of close attention. You will learn to research without drowning, to interview without interrogating, to structure a life without flattening it, and to write sentences that sound like a person, not a résumé. By the end you will have drafted a five-hundred-word portrait of someone real.

02 — Modules

Six movements, from blank page to finished portrait.

Module 01

The Premise

What makes a life worth writing? We read short portraits by Plutarch, Boswell, and Hermione Lee to notice what the writer chooses — and what they leave out.

  • Reading: Eminent Victorians, ch. 1
  • Exercise: 200-word pitch
  • Time: 60 min
Module 02

Research Without Drowning

Primary vs. secondary sources, archives, public records, and the etiquette of asking a family for letters. How to take notes you will actually be able to find again.

  • Reading: Tuchman, “In Search of History”
  • Exercise: Source map
  • Time: 90 min
Module 03

The Interview

Preparing questions that open rather than close. Silence as a tool. Transcribing, verifying, and — most important — knowing when to stop.

  • Reading: Terkel, Working (intro)
  • Exercise: One 30-min interview
  • Time: 2 hrs
Module 04

Structure & Shape

Chronology is the obvious choice, and often the wrong one. Spiral, mosaic, single day, single object — choosing a shape that fits the life you are writing.

  • Reading: Ginzburg, Family Lexicon (excerpt)
  • Exercise: Outline in 9 beats
  • Time: 60 min
Module 05

Voice & Scene

Making a stranger feel alive on the page. Concrete detail, dialogue, and the ethics of imagined interior life. When to show; when to say.

  • Reading: Malcolm, The Silent Woman
  • Exercise: One scene, 250 words
  • Time: 75 min
Module 06

Revision & Responsibility

Fact-checking yourself. What your subject gets to read before publication. Cutting your favorite sentence. Shipping.

  • Reading: Lee, “How to End It”
  • Exercise: Final 500-word draft
  • Time: 90 min
03 — Methods

Three habits that separate a biography from a summary.

Habit 01

Collect more than you need.

For every fact you will use, gather five you will not. The ones you discard still shape the tone of the ones you keep.

Habit 02

Write for the specific, not the general.

“A woman” is nobody. “A woman who kept cigarettes in a tin labeled Assam” is already alive.

Habit 03

Let the subject surprise you.

If the person on the page only confirms what you already believed, you have written an opinion, not a biography. Revise until something in them resists you.

04 — Workshop

Write your opening paragraph, right now.

The hardest sentence in any biography is the first. Use the prompts on the right to sketch an opening for someone you know — a grandparent, a neighbor, a friend — in under five minutes.

Don't try to cover their whole life. Pick a single detail, a single room, a single morning. The rest of the life will arrive through the door you open.
